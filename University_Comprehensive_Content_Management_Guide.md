## Content Strategy: Overview and Introduction

### What is Content Strategy

- Content strategy guides the creation, delivery, and governance of useful, usable content.
- Content strategy means getting the right content, to the right people, in the right place, at the right time.

*Source: Kristina Halvorson, Brain Traffic*

### Purpose of This Guide

This guide is a collaboration between University Marketing and Communications and IT Solutions, designed to help you understand your audience, organize, assess, and manage public or shared content within your area.

Effective content management is a continuous process of organizing, evaluating, and assigning materials to ensure they are accessible, relevant, and aligned with the needs of our University community. This includes meeting digital accessibility standards for all users.

### Why This is Important

As areas work to understand and organize their content, it's essential to keep the user at the forefront. Students, faculty, staff, and other stakeholders need content that is useful, usable, relevant, accessible and easy to navigate.

#### What Are Digital Accessibility Standards?

When we say content needs to be accessible, we mean it must be usable by everyone — including people with disabilities. This includes individuals who use screen readers, rely on keyboard navigation, or have visual, auditory, cognitive, or motor impairments.

We follow the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 AA**, which outline how to make digital content:

- **Perceivable** – content can be seen or heard, regardless of ability
- **Operable** – users can navigate and interact with content using various tools
- **Understandable** – information is clear and readable
- **Robust** – content works across different devices and assistive technologies

By meeting these standards, we ensure our content is inclusive and usable for all users. View resources on making your content accessible on the [Digital Accessibility Hub](https://mnscu.sharepoint.com/sites/MNSU-Fountain/SitePages/Digital-Accessibility-Hub.aspx) on The Fountain.

### What This Guide Will Help Areas Achieve

Following steps in this Content Strategy guide will help areas:

- Clarify content goals based on audience.
- Establish content review workflows.
- Maintain up-to-date, accessible content that meets compliance standards.
- Shift from outdated formats (like PDFs) to streamlined digital solutions.

## Overview of MSU's Main Content Platforms

The University has several platforms designed to organize, share, communicate, and manage content efficiently. Each platform serves a different purpose and audience, ensuring that content is accessible to the right people, in the right format, and at the right time. While each of these serves as the primary home for content, there is a lot of interlinking and promotion/call outs to these platforms as part of most plans.

---

### 1. Website

**Intended for:** External audience for marketing, recruitment, connection, and communication.

**Primary Audience:** Prospective students and influencers
- Undergraduate
- Graduate
- International
- Admitted
- Online

**Secondary Audience:** Current students, alumni/donors, faculty, staff, community members, and the general public.

The website is the University's main public platform, providing essential information and showcasing academic programs, admissions, campus life, financial aid, and news for prospective students, families, and the community.

**What Content Goes Here:**
- Admissions information
- Paying for College information
- Housing and Dining information
- Academics, program descriptions and academic catalog
- A gateway to Student Support Resources and Campus Life
- Events and University news
- Alumni achievements, Giving, Become a Partner, Community Engagement, About MSU

**Why It's Important:** The website plays a central role in attracting and informing potential students, faculty, staff, and external stakeholders. It must be well-organized, engaging, and easy to navigate to ensure prospective students have a positive first experience with the University.

**Governance & Roles:**
- **Ownership:** University Marketing and Communication in partnership with IT Solutions
- **Day to Day Owners:** Director of Web Marketing and IT Solutions Web Applications Developer
- **High-Stakeholders:** AVP of University Marketing and Communication, ACIO for Solutions Delivery, President Inch, VP of IT Solutions

**Primary Audience:** Faculty, staff, and emeriti.

**Purpose:** The Fountain is the University's internal communications platform built on SharePoint. ## Content Audit & Transition Step-by-Step Guide

This guide outlines the process for understanding audience needs, digital content review and the content migration process.

Following the nine steps ensures content is accurate, accessible, and aligned with University standards, while being placed on the most appropriate platform for its audience and purpose. Each step clearly notes who leads, who supports, and when to bring in additional expertise — making the transition smooth and keeping content high-quality long after go-live.

### Key Roles

- **Area Project Lead (APL):** Main representative(s) for a department, division or area. One is recommended, but there may be up to two. This person could be a content expert themselves or someone who works internally with their area's staff to gather needed information.
- **Content Strategy Team:** ITS leadership and communications along with University Marketing & Communications leadership and director of web marketing; provides process guidance, content strategy, and governance oversight.
- **Web Team:** University Marketing & Communications director of web marketing and IT Solutions web applications developer; provides content rewrites, webpage design, enforces digital accessibility and quality assurance. The web applications developer also assists with technical support, migration, and platform setup.
- **OneStop IT Administrator (TeamDynamix Expert):** Available as needed to address technical "back-end" considerations for Maverick OneStop and other TeamDynamix-supported processes.

### Internal Audit and Analytics Review

Before engaging areas, the Content Strategy and Web Teams will:

- Pull existing content from the area's webpages.
- Conduct a content audit using analytics (e.g., page views, search terms).
- Identify content gaps, redundancies, and accessibility issues.

The audit will be documented in a shared markup-ready format, which will be shared with the area for review.

---

### Step 1: Project Kickoff Meeting

**Lead:** Content Strategy Team  
**Involved:** Area Project Lead  
**As Needed:** OneStop IT Administrator, Web Applications Developer

The kickoff meeting sets the foundation for the entire project. The Content Strategy Team will:

- Set expectations
- Introduce roles
- Share initial content audit and analytics
- Outline the area's review responsibilities

The content audit and analytics will be shared at the meeting. These usage reports highlight what's working, what's underused, and potential gaps to guide later steps.

---

### Step 2: User Journey

**Lead:** Area Project Lead  
**Support:** Comms Team (guidance)

A user's journey is a way to step into someone else's shoes — like a student, parent, or faculty member — and think through what high-level task they're trying to do and how they go about doing it.

As you map your user journeys, refer to any available usage reports from Step 1 to see what your audiences are actually searching for, visiting most, or struggling to find. These insights can help confirm priorities or reveal unexpected needs.

**Creating a few user journeys helps you better align your content with real user needs — because users rarely need everything at once.**

It's like telling a short story about:
- Who the person is
- What they're trying to do
- Why it matters to them
- Where they go to find help (website, D2L, email, phone, etc.)
- When they need to access the information and at what point in an academic year
- What gets in their way or makes it easier

#### A Simple Template to Use

> As a [type of person], I want to [do something], so that I can [reach my goal].

**Examples:**
- As a *prospective student*, I want to find out how to apply for financial aid, so that I can afford to attend MSU.
- As a *current student*, I want to know which classes I need to graduate, so that I can stay on track and finish my degree on time.
- As a *prospective master's student*, I want to find an accredited public health program, so that I can qualify to take the licensure exam and advance my career in health policy.
- As a *new faculty member*, I want to know how to set up my course in the D2L learning management system, so that I can be ready for the first day of class.

#### How to Begin Mapping a User Journey

1. Pick one audience (e.g., prospective student, parent, faculty).
2. Think of one common task they try to do with your area.
3. Use the sentence template to describe their journey.

Ask yourself:
- Where do they go first? (Website? Email?)
- What questions do they have?
- What might confuse them?
- How can we make it easier?

**Conducting this user journey activity will help you:**
- Better understand your audience's real needs
- Identify gaps or confusing parts in your current content or processes
- Make it easier for people to get what they need from your area
- Create a more welcoming and effective experience for everyone
- Spot content that no longer helps your users — so you can update it, fix it, or remove it

### Step 3: Area Review and Markup

**Lead:** Area Project Lead  
**Support:** Content Strategy Team

Areas will:

- Review the audited content and **markup** what is:
  - Outdated
  - Inaccurate
  - Missing
- Describe their **audience(s)** for each content area based on information from the Step 2 user journey exercise.
- Indicate **when** their audience needs the information (e.g., during application, advising, financial aid, onboarding).
- Provide any compliance or accreditation requirements that affect content placement or visibility.

> If your area's content is subject to accreditation, regulatory, or compliance standards, those requirements must be submitted to the content strategy team to ensure alignment and proper platform assignment.

This step ensures that the Content Strategy and Web Teams understand the context and timing of content use from the area's perspective.

---

### Step 4: Content Strategy Evaluation

**Lead:** Content Strategy Team  
**Support:** Web Team

The Content Strategy Team will:

- Review the area's markup and audience descriptions.
- Determine content priorities and platform placement.
- Identify opportunities to consolidate, streamline, or remove content.

This step ensures alignment with MSU's content strategy goals, brand voice and tone, accessibility standards, quality standards, and platform governance.

---

### Step 5: Web Content Rewrite and Page Design

**Lead:** Web Team  
**Support:** Content Strategy Team

The Web Team will:

- Rewrite content using web-writing and user experience (UX) best practices.
- Apply accessibility and UX standards.
- Design new page layouts and structure based on user needs and platform guidelines.

Areas will not be responsible for rewriting content but will have the opportunity to review and provide feedback.

---

### Step 6: Area Review of Built Webpages

**Lead:** Area Project Lead  
**Support:** Content Strategy Team + Web Team

Once webpages are built (but not live), areas will:

- Review the new content and layout.
- Provide feedback within a **2-week turnaround**.
- Participate in walkthroughs or conversations to clarify decisions and discuss analytics insights.

This is a collaborative checkpoint to ensure accuracy, alignment, and usability before launching.

---

### Step 7: Content Movement

**Lead:** Web Team (for mnsu.edu), Area Project Lead (for OneStop, MavLife, Teams)  
**Support:** Content Strategy Team and University Marketing and Communications Team (quality check)  
**Involved:** Area Project Lead (for non-web platforms), OneStop IT Administrator

Once content is rewritten and approved:

- The Web Team will migrate content to the new mnsu.edu site.
- For other platforms (e.g., Maverick OneStop, The Fountain, MavLife), areas may submit tickets or work with platform-specific contacts to move content.
- Redirects and linking strategies will be coordinated by the Web Team to ensure seamless user experience across platforms.

> **Note:** If content lives on one platform but needs visibility elsewhere, the Web Team will guide cross-linking and call-to-action placement.

---

### Step 8: Go Live

**Lead:** Web Team + Content Strategy Team  
**Support:** Area

Once content is migrated and reviewed:

- The Web Team will coordinate the technical launch.
- ITS Web Applications Developer will coordinate the redirects.
- The Content Strategy Team will perform a final quality check for branding, accessibility, and usability.
- Areas will be notified of the go-live and asked to:
  - Confirm accuracy of their content.
  - Communicate updates through service management as needed.
  - Update any related documents or links that reference the old content.

---

### Step 9: Maintenance

**Lead:** Area  
**Support:** Content Strategy Team

After launching, areas are responsible for keeping their content accurate and relevant. To support this:

- Schedule annual content reviews.
- Submit website content update tickets as needed for changes.
- The Content Strategy Team will provide guidance and support for ongoing governance, accessibility, and platform alignment.

---

### Tips for Success

- **Prioritize High-Impact Content:** Focus on the most frequently accessed content or content that answers common questions or needs for your audience.
- **Keep Content Up-to-Date and Relevant:** Regularly review content to ensure it is still accurate and useful.
- **Avoid Overloading Users with Too Much Information:** Break down complex information into scannable, concise "chunks."
- **Be Consistent Across Platforms, but Recognize Platform Differences:** Adapt the organization of content based on the platform's audience and purpose (e.g., Website vs. Maverick OneStop vs. The Fountain).
- **Collaborate Across Teams for Unified Strategy:** Work together with other teams to ensure consistency and clarity across areas.

## Digital Forms Recommendation

As you go through this process, it's an ideal time to digitize your forms and processes. Many forms and workflows currently rely on PDFs, paper processes, or require emailing specific people, which can lead to confusion, delays, accessibility issues, or lost information.

**IT Solutions** can help streamline these processes by setting up digital forms and workflow automation that will:

- Make tasks faster and more efficient
- Provide clear visibility into where each process stands
- Reduce the likelihood of getting lost, missed, or making errors
- Save time for everyone involved

Contact IT Solutions to help transition your processes to digital solutions that are easier to manage and track.

## Content That Does Not Fit into University Platforms

Not all content belongs on our University platforms. As part of this strategy, content that does not align with our core platforms' purpose, audience, or accessibility standards will not be carried over.

When this occurs:

- PDF copies of the content can be provided back to the area for their records.
- We may suggest outside platforms or tools (e.g., LinkedIn, WordPress, professional association sites) that may be more appropriate for the content's purpose and audience.

This approach helps keep University platforms clear, consistent, and effective, while still supporting areas in finding a home for their information elsewhere.
